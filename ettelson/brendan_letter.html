When I was overseas I made a little over US$1600 (after commissions and fees) in a couple of currency transfers. I continued to make smaller sums in other weighted currency shenanigans. The total amount of time required was very small; the single biggest item was the five minute walk to the bank from my office.

I guess if I wasn't making much money at the time or (worse) did not have a job at all I would have felt inadequate and discouraged. I imagine I would have turned to these minor deals to boost my spirits. It would have been fun to call myself an "International Currency Speculator" or some such, and had business cards printed, and bragged about how much I made per hour. And all this would have been a good way to get my morale up to find better ways to make money with my time and skills, ($1600 is all very well and good, but was still less than a week's pay as an assignee in '93. And an assignee's salary depends much less on the mercurial whims of Ms Luck.) The title "International Currency Speculator" would have been doubly splendid because the best are indeed very wealthy, and people who don't know me might assume I am another Soros or something. I would be able bear the trappings of success without having to actually succeed.

All this would be good if I used it to energize myself into finding a business or job that paid well. Self-esteem is, after all, very important while hunting for a new occupation or enterprise. Still, using the above method to boost self-esteem must be weighed against the downsides.

The first is that people don't like to be deceived. If I talk to new acquaintances as if I am involved in big international deals and such. and then they discover that I made only a few thousand last year, well, they'll think I'm a shyster or a megalomaniac or both. This is even more perilous with old friends because they will tire of my prattle about Big Deals that never seem to work out, They will think that either a) I am being deliberately deceptive and self-aggrandizing, or b) that I am mad and have lost the ability to distinguish between success and the illusion of success. Only my best (or most patient) friends would put up with me for long.

The more dangerous downside is I might start to believe these splendid titles myself, and allow myself to get distracted into putting effort into activities because they would sound impressive at cocktail parties and not because they would be profitable.
